This week in Learning & Development -

AI is dominating every boardroom conversation right now, but it raises a tough question: are we giving our people the actual support they need to keep pace?

From creeping capability anxiety and tightening green card pipelines to evolving manager responsibilities, this week, we take a close look at a growing workplace disconnect.

While AI ambition is soaring, everyday coaching and skill development are quietly slipping. Employees want to grow, but many are left guessing which skills will keep them relevant, and manager-led development is on the decline.

Technology alone won't secure our employees' future; equipping, coaching, and supporting the human workforce will.

Until next week.

- Deepa Damodaran
Editor, Leadership & Employee Development Excellence Newsletter

 
The Big Shift

Four Workforce Trends Show Development Support Falling Behind AI Ambition

By Deepa Damodaran | HR.com

Employees know their work is changing, but many are uncertain about which skills will keep them relevant.

New research from WTW, edX Enterprise, Workday, Gartner and Wiley points to four trends: uncertainty about what to learn, AI priorities outpacing organizational readiness, pressure on career mobility and manager support, and unclear accountability for AI.

The share of employees who say their managers actively help develop their abilities has fallen to 78% from 84% over four years. Meanwhile, only 24% of leadership teams agree on who is accountable for AI agents.

Are organizations giving employees the development support they need to keep pace with changing work?

Read the full story to see what five recent studies reveal about AI skills, career development and the support employees need to adapt.
On the Pulse

Tech Green Card Freeze Tests Talent Pipelines

The Labor Department suspended Microsoft, Adobe and six IT firms from the PERM step that precedes most employer-sponsored green cards, leaving some employees’ paths to permanent residency on hold with no end date. Existing H-1B status is unaffected.

For organizations, it raises questions about how managers handle employee concerns, what happens to hiring and training at affected companies, and whether reskilling records matter as scrutiny of employer practices widens. Read More.

Learning: Reality Check

Employees Know They Need New Skills. Many Don't Know Which Ones.

Employees know their jobs are changing. What many cannot tell is which skills will keep them relevant as artificial intelligence reshapes their roles. WTW calls this uncertainty “capability anxiety” and identifies it as an emerging workforce risk in its 2026 Employee Experience Global Market Study.

Managers are best placed to help, yet the share of workers who say their manager actively develops their abilities has dropped to 78% from 84% over four years. Meanwhile, fewer than a third of employers have a process to track workforce skills, even as 45% of employees say AI is integral to their daily work.

Read the article to learn about WTW’s four recommendations for closing the skills clarity gap.

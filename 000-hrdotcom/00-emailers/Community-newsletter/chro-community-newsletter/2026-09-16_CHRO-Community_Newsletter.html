Top Story

Why the CHRO Must Lead Transformation, Not Just Staff It

In conversation with HR expert Dan Kaplan

By Babitha Balakrishnan | HR.com

The strategy is set. The technology is chosen. The operating model is taking shape. The business knows where it wants to go.

Then the CHRO is brought in to answer a critical question: What does all of this mean for our people?

That sequence is the problem.

A CHRO who's expected to lead transformation can't be brought in after the direction is locked. Decisions about technology, operating models, leadership, and the future of work will inevitably reshape the workforce, making the CHRO’s perspective relevant from the start.

That doesn't mean the CHRO owns every technology or transformation decision. But when the workforce leader enters the conversation only after roles need filling and skills need patching, that's not transformation leadership; it's cleanup, says HR expert Dan Kaplan of ZRG Partners.

👉 Read the article to learn why Kaplan believes bringing the CHRO into strategy formation earlier makes all the difference.

Research Radar

HR Ranks No. 1 for Job Security: Here Are the 10 Most and Least Secure Jobs

If job security had a leaderboard, HR would be sitting at the top.

Zety’s 2026 Job Security Index puts human resources managers at No. 1, based on millions of employee-reported reviews and worker sentiment metrics. And it’s not just one HR role making the cut: another HR role also ranks among the 10 most secure jobs.

What makes some jobs feel more secure than others? The answer goes beyond the industry or job title. The nature of the work itself matters, including how much a role depends on problem-solving, technology, physical activity and specialized preparation.

The bottom of the ranking paints a very different picture, with some occupations facing a much lower sense of job security despite the skills and preparation they require.

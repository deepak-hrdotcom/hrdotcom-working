Top Story

AI Agents Are Becoming More Autonomous. What Does That Mean For HR?

By Babitha Balakrishnan | HR.com

AI agents have been making news for a very specific reason: they’re starting to push beyond the boundaries humans set for them.

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta have all reported incidents involving AI agents bypassing controls or accessing systems they weren’t supposed to reach.

For technology companies, highly autonomous agents present a fundamental safety and security challenge. For everyone using or developing these systems, they also raise a broader question: Where should the boundaries be, and who is responsible for setting them?

That question is equally relevant to HR leaders today as AI agents take on more work involving employees, workforce data and HR decisions.

Matt Jones, Executive Vice President of Strategy at Cielo, looks at the bigger implications of agentic AI for HR, from deciding what AI should be allowed to do and where accountability belongs to how organizations redesign work, rethink workforce capabilities and preserve human judgment where it matters most. His perspective brings the discussion beyond AI adoption to the choices HR leaders will need to make as agents become capable of taking on more complex work.

Up Close with HR Leaders

The Question HR Can’t Avoid Anymore: What Does Growth Look Like Now?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is now part of most enterprise strategies, but the harder question is how it actually moves through an organization.

Plans are clear at the top. What happens next depends on how those plans travel through layers of roles, systems, and day-to-day work. That movement is rarely smooth. It requires coordination between teams that are already operating under existing priorities, structures, and constraints.

This is where the gap appears. Not in the intention behind AI adoption, but in how it is translated into execution across the organization.

In a conversation with Wesley Cherisien, AI implementation strategist and founder of Learnytics, this gap comes into focus as he breaks down where AI strategy is failing, how middle management is being reshaped, and what organizations must rethink for adoption to work in practice.

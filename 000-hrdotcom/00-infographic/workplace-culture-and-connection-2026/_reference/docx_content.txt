Workplace Culture and Connection 2026
Make culture thrive by fostering communication, camaraderie, and trust at all levels of the organization.
Organizations need to work on culture and connection
Connection is the foundation of workplace culture and one of the strongest reasons people stay
83% of employees say they remain with an organization primarily because of culture and the people they work with
71% say they feel a sense of belonging at their organization
However, many organizations continue to face challenges
20% hesitate to recommend their organization as a great place to work
Nearly 1 in 5 report their sense of connection declined over the past year
48% report only moderate or low trust in leadership
On the surface, things look good, but connection across the organization is also uneven 
86% feel connected to their immediate team
Yet only 58% say the organization fosters relationships beyond those teams
So how can organizations improve workplace culture and connection?
First, determine your biggest culture and connection obstacles
Cultural strain rarely shows up as a single issue. It accumulates through everyday experiences.
When determining the biggest culture and connection obstacles, employees most often point to:
lack of clear communication and transparency
not feeling recognized or valued
change fatigue and declining morale
Second, show that leadership cares

When leadership connection is visible, trust grows. When it is not, even well-intentioned culture efforts lose credibility.

There is a disconnect in perception of top leadership involvement. Executives are much more likely than managers to believe the C-suite is involved in improving the work environment to a high or very high degree:
CHROs: Whereas 71% of Chief HR Executives believe they are highly involved in improving the work environment, only 54% of managers believe their CHROs are highly involved
The same dynamic is seen with CEOs (69% vs. 52%) and with CFOs (60% vs. 39%)

Moreover, only 16% of employees and managers trust leadership to a very high extent

Compared to culture laggard organizations, culture leader organizations are:
8X more likely to report high trust in leadership (83% vs. 10%)
over 3X more likely to say the CHRO takes an active role in improving the work environment (70% vs. 22%)
over 3X more likely to say the CEO takes an active role in improving the work environment (70% vs. 22%)

Third, ensure you’re using tools to build relationships and skills

A third of organizations aren’t measuring employee engagement, making it difficult to overcome culture & connection challenges.
And most aren’t using the following to help build relationships, including:
culture/engagement consultants: 82%
Employee Resource Groups (ERGs): 73%
coaching/mentoring programs: 68%
well-being/wellness apps: 61%
Not to mention,
59% of managers and executives do not know their organization’s eNPS
54% do not know voluntary turnover rates
Without this visibility, it is difficult to understand whether employees feel connected, valued, or willing to advocate for the organization.
Fourth, reinforce connection through consistent recognition
Recognition is one of the most direct ways employees experience connection, yet it’s inconsistent across many organizations.
37% of employees rarely or never receive meaningful recognition from their direct manager
35% rarely or never receive meaningful recognition from peers
Compared to employees in culture laggard organizations, those in culture leader organizations are:
16X more likely to receive weekly or daily recognition from managers (94% vs. 6%)
9X more likely to receive frequent peer recognition (95% vs. 10%)



Final Key Takeaway
Connection is not just a nice-to-have. It is how culture produces trust, engagement, and results. Organizations that track connection, recognize it when it happens, and show visible leadership consistently perform better than those that don't. When you treat connection like a system rather than a feeling, culture becomes something you can build on, not something you just hope holds together.
About the Survey:
 The HR Research Institute (HRRI) fielded "The State of Workplace Culture and Connection 2025-26" survey in October 2025 in collaboration with Motivosity. We gathered 5,538 total responses from participants located worldwide, though predominantly from North America. Participants span diverse industries, with 80% representing midsize or large organizations.

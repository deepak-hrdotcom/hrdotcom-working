Substance Use, Mental Health, and Executive Risk: Hidden Liabilities in the Workplace

Organizations routinely plan for financial, legal, and operational risk, yet one of the most significant and least addressed threats to leadership continuity and organizational stability often remains unspoken: untreated substance use and co-occurring mental health challenges aamong executives and key personnel whose roles may be critical to organizational success.

At the highest level of business, substance use disorder presents a unique paradox. Individuals in executive and key positions often have greater autonomy, resources, and influence, allowing challenges to be minimized, concealed, or accommodated for longer periods. When intervention does occur, it is frequently routed through high-cost, often “luxury” treatment environments that prioritize discretion and comfort, but may lack the accountability and longitudinal care required for sustained recovery. This can result in repeated treatment cycles, inconsistent performance, and ongoing organizational risk.

Drawing on decades of clinical experience treating high-functioning professionals, executives, and key organizational stakeholders, this presentation will examine how these conditions translate into material financial, operational, legal, and reputational liabilities for companies and how those liabilities can be mitigated by timely, skilled intervention.

This session is presented by the clinical team at Recovery Without Walls, an integrative outpatient medical practice specializing in addiction, chronic pain, and complex psychiatric care. The practice is led by Howard Kornfeld, MD, a nationally recognized addiction and pain specialist with more than four decades of clinical experience, including long-standing faculty involvement at UCSF and early leadership in compassionate outpatient treatment models. The broader team includes physicians, psychiatric providers, and care coordinators experienced in working with high-functioning professionals and executives requiring discretion, continuity, and highly individualized care.

Helping Organizational Leadership Understand 'Future Value' in Payroll

Payroll sits at the center of the employment relationship, yet the way organizations measure its impact remains narrowly defined. Most leadership teams still view payroll through the lens of cost, efficiency, and compliance. These metrics are at once precise, defensible, and deeply incomplete. This bias toward what is easily measured obscures payroll’s growing influence on trust, retention, workforce stability, and overall organizational performance.

This session challenges the prevailing financial and operational frameworks that limit how payroll’s value is understood. It introduces three practical ways payroll leaders can help executive stakeholders see beyond cost accounting and begin recognizing payroll as a driver of future value:

-> First, we reframe efficiency as a starting point, not an outcome. We do so by exposing how traditional metrics capture immediate financial outputs while missing downstream effects on engagement, performance, and retention.

-> Second, we explore how to translate payroll’s impact into the language of finance without losing its human dimension, bridging the gap between GAAP-aligned reporting and the realities of workforce value creation.

-> Third, we examine how payroll data, when connected to broader workforce and business outcomes, can tell a more complete story about the conditions under which employees do their best work.

Attendees will leave with a clearer understanding of why payroll’s most strategic contributions often go unrecognized and how to change that narrative. More importantly, they will gain a practical framework for helping leadership account not just for what payroll costs, but for what it makes possible.

The Questions Nobody Asks…Until the Culture Change Fails

Let's be honest, most culture change efforts don't fail because of a bad strategy. They fail because nobody stopped to ask whether the organization was ready before the initiative launched.

I've seen it happen more times than I can count. Leaders get energized, a new values framework gets rolled out, the engagement survey goes live, and a year later, nothing has really shifted. Not because people didn't care, but because the hard questions never got asked upfront.

That’s what this roundtable is about — not a framework, not a presentation, but a real conversation among experienced HR leaders about what readiness for culture change looks like, and how the questions we ask (or avoid) early on can make or break the whole effort.
What does it mean for leaders to be truly aligned versus just enthusiastic?

How do you find out what's really going on when people don't feel safe enough to tell you the truth?

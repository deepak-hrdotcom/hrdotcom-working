# **Why Should You Get HR Certified?**

## *Your Career Deserves Better Than Hoping for the Best*

Have you ever noticed how many of the human resources professionals in management and leadership roles have a PHR, SPHR, SHRM-CP or SHRM-SCP after their name?

There's a reason for that. Actually, quite a few\!

And this quick guide is here to cover six of the most important ones for you, and help you better understand why getting certified is planning for success, rather than merely hoping for the best.

## **1\. Show Me the Money (Because We All Have Bills)**

**Career Growth & Higher Salary:** Here's a fun fact that'll make your wallet happy: a PayScale study found that certified HR professionals earn on average $10,000–$20,000 more per year than their non-certified peers. That's more than just "treat yourself to fancy coffee" money.

Yes, quality instructor-led exam preparation might cost around $2,000, but let's do the math: if you earn even an extra $10,000 annually, you've paid for that investment five times over in just one year.

## **2\. Stand Out in a Sea of Sameness**

**Competitive Edge:** Picture a recruiter staring at 200 resumes that all blur together. Then suddenly, there it is: your certification shining like a beacon of competence. Most supervisors prefer certified candidates, which means you're not just another resume in the pile — you're the top choice.

## **3\. Earn Your Stripes (And Keeping Them)**

**Credibility & Confidence:** HR certifications demonstrate validated knowledge in compliance, employee relations, and talent management. It's the difference between being seen as someone who handles HR tasks versus someone who understands HR strategy. When colleagues and leadership need guidance on complex workforce issues, they're more likely to turn to someone whose expertise has been independently validated.

You'll find yourself speaking with more authority in meetings and decision-making situations. The certification process builds genuine confidence because you know your recommendations are grounded in proven frameworks and best practices, not just gut instinct.

## **4\. Enjoy the Sweet Taste of Achievement**

**Personal Achievement:** Passing a rigorous HR certification exam requires studying and hard work. You'll spend evenings and weekends preparing when you could be binge-watching Netflix. But here's the thing: anything worth having requires effort, and the satisfaction of conquering a challenging exam will give you unmatched confidence.

## **5\. See How Knowledge Is Power**

**Build Skills:** The certification study process isn't just about memorizing facts for a test — it's about genuinely expanding your professional toolkit. You'll dive deep into areas you might have only touched on before, connecting dots you didn't even know existed.

Here's the reality: you might be good at your job, maybe even passionate about it, but that doesn't always translate to career advancement. Certification bridges that gap by providing measurable proof of your capabilities. This isn't theoretical knowledge that looks pretty on paper but crumbles under pressure. It's practical, applicable expertise that makes you better at your job from day one.

## **6\. Avoid Having Regrets. Plan for Results Instead.**

**No Regrets:** Career opportunities don't usually come with a "do-over" button. How many times have you watched a colleague get promoted and thought, "That could have been me if only I had..." Well, certification eliminates that particular flavor of professional regret.

Getting certified gives you the best possible chance to succeed. You're removing variables and stacking the deck in your favor. Years from now, when you're in that better position or leading that team, you won't be wondering "what if." You'll be enjoying the results of the investment you made in yourself.

## **The Bottom Line**

HR certification isn't just a nice-to-have credential; it's a strategic investment in your career that typically pays dividends in terms of opportunities and compensation. It's the difference between hoping your experience speaks for itself and having concrete evidence of your expertise.

So ask yourself: do you want to keep explaining why you deserve opportunities, or do you want to start choosing between them? In the world of HR, certification isn't just about proving you know your stuff — it's about positioning yourself for the growth you've been working toward.

The investment in yourself is real. The potential returns are too.

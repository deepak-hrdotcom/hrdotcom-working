FAQs on HR certification

Answers to the most common questions about credentials, exam bodies, and preparation.

What are the primary HR certification providers and options?

+

The two main organizations offering recognized HR certification are the Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM) and the HR Certification Institute (HRCI).

  • SHRM offers the SHRM-CP (Certified Professional) and SHRM-SCP (Senior Certified Professional).
  • HRCI offers the aPHR (Associate), PHR (Professional), SPHR (Senior Professional), and international designations (PHRi, SPHRi).

What percent of HR professionals are certified?

+

Payscale surveys indicate approximately 34.2% of HR professionals hold at least one certification. Among senior HR leaders (Managers, Directors, VPs, CHROs), certification rates exceed 50%.

What are the main advantages of having HR certification?

+

The primary documented career advantages include:

  • Higher Compensation: Certified HR professionals earn an average of $10,000–$20,000+ more per year.
  • Increased Job Opportunities: Top employers filter resumes by credential status.
  • Improved Job Performance: Deepens practical mastery of federal laws, compliance, and strategic HR management.
  • Faster Promotion: Certified professionals are 2.5× more likely to be promoted to executive roles.

How long does it take to prepare for an HR certification exam?

+

We recommend a minimum of 4 to 6 months of structured study (approximately 6–8 hours per week) to ensure comprehensive mastery and eliminate exam day anxiety.

How does HR recertification work?

+

HR professionals must recertify every three years to maintain active status, typically by earning 60 pre-approved recertification credits (45 for aPHR) through webcasts, conferences, and courses — or by retaking the exam.

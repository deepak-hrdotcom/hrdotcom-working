 Finding: Just over a third of managers are
skilled at setting goals
 To be good at performance management, managers need to be good
at setting goals and having conversations about performance and
behaviors. Most managers are not strong in those areas. Only 36% are
skilled at setting goals, and only 29% are skilled at having conversations
about performance and behaviors. This lack of skill explains why only
31% of managers were rated as skilled in performance management.
One reason most managers lack the necessary skills is likely a lack
 of sufficient training; only 37% have received adequate training in
 performance management. SHRM research found that 61% of HR
professionals say fewer than half of their managers effectively address
underperformance with direct reports, and 41% say their organization
 does not provide adequate training and resources for managers to
 conduct effective performance reviews.

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 Based on our research, please consider the following suggestions:
 1. Build managersʼ core performance management skills.
 Only 36% of managers are skilled at setting goals and 29% at leading performance and behavior
 conversations, largely because just 37% have received adequate training at all. Targeted programs in
goal setting, coaching conversations, developmental planning, and difficult conversations close these
specific gaps directly, rather than relying on generic leadership training.
 2. Close the baseline accountability gap before adding new metrics.
 23% of organizations hold managers accountable in no specific way at all. Before building out new KPIs
 or measurement systems, make sure every manager has at least one accountability mechanism in
place. The gap between "some accountability" and "none" is one of the sharpest dividing lines between
leader and laggard organizations.
 3. Once accountability exists, measure outcomes and feedback, not just completion.
 Appraisal completion is still the most common accountability method (47%), but it only confirms tasks
got done, not whether performance management is actually working. PM leaders go further: they build
performance responsibilities into managersʼ formal KPIs and promotion criteria, evaluate managers
on their direct reportsʼ engagement and retention and incorporate employee feedback into manager
 assessments. These outcomes and feedback based measures capture real managerial effectiveness.

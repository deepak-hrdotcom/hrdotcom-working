 Finding: Learning, communication, and
organizational requirements are among the
top objectives of performance management
 We asked respondents about the objectives of their performance
 management processes. One of the striking features of performance
management is that it is meant to achieve many different goals. The top
 five objectives are:
 ●   help individual employees learn and grow
 ●   meet organizational requirements (e.g., goals, compliance)
 ●   boost communication between employees and their managers
 ●   help the organization improve overall performance
 ●   boost the individual performance of employees
 These objectives tend to be mutually reinforcing, that is, progress on one
typically drives progress on the others. Organizations donʼt necessarily
 need to choose between these goals, but can instead design a single,
 well-integrated process that advances all five simultaneously.

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 Based on our research, please consider the following suggestions:
 1. Connect employee goals to enterprise priorities.
 Among organizations without a culture conducive to performance leadership, limited alignment
between individual goals and business priorities is one of the most commonly cited barriers. Making
 that link explicit and visible is also how HR earns senior leadershipʼs confidence that PM is more than
 an administrative exercise.
 2. Regularly diagnose where the PM process is breaking down.
 Among organizations struggling with performance leadership culture, barriers are numerous and
often compounding—lack of training, manager time constraints, resistance to change, and poor
communication all rank highly. Build in a regular audit of the process, by team, by manager, by outcome,
so HR can target interventions.
 3. Design a single, integrated PM process rather than a patchwork of initiatives.
 Five distinct objectives (learning and growth, communication, organizational requirements, org
 performance, and individual performance) are each cited by over 60% of organizations, and these goals
tend to reinforce one another. Rather than running separate efforts for each, build one well-integrated
process that advances all of them together.

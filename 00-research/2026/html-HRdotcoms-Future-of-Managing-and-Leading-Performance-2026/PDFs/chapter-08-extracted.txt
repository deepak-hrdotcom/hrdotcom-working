 Future Trends in PM
 Finding: Organizations most commonly say
performance management in the future will
include more manager training
 Whatʼs predicted to happen in the world of PM over the next two years?
A majority of organizations anticipate performance management
will include more manager training (60%). This will help address the
issue where managers are often promoted for excelling as individual
contributors, but arenʼt prepared to lead, coach, or evaluate others.
Without dedicated training, many are left to run reviews, feedback
conversations, and goal setting on instinct rather than a defined skill set.
About half also believe PM will become more aligned with business
goals (52%) and incorporate more frequent or continuous feedback
(53%) over the next two years. Development-focused change (52%)
follows closely behind, suggesting a strong shift toward proactive,
growth-oriented practices rather than purely evaluative ones. AI-
enablement (43%) and skills-based approaches (40%) also register
meaningfully, pointing to emerging shifts in both technology and
philosophy. Fewer organizations expect change centered on employee
experience and well-being (32%) or individual personalization (28%),
suggesting these areas may lag behind more structural or process-driven
changes.
Only 5% expect no change at all, and just 1% believe performance
management will disappear entirely, indicating near-universal consensus
that evolution is coming, even if organizations differ on where to focus
first.

 Finding: PM technology is centered around
reviews and goal tracking
 Most organizations use technology to facilitate performance reviews
 (74%) and track goals (72%), but far fewer use it to support learning
and development (31%). Analytics and reporting (49%) and evaluating
 performance against goals (47%) also rank fairly high, reinforcing that
 these systems are primarily built for evaluation rather than growth.
 Notably, two of the top three goals of performance reviews are to
help employees learn and grow and to boost communication, so
 there remains a serious disconnect between the goals of PM and the
capabilities of most systems used by responding organizations.
Closing this gap will likely require organizations to prioritize
 development- and communication-focused features when selecting or
 upgrading PM technology.
Craig Friedman, Sr. Talent Advisor and Skills Practice Leader at St.
Charles Consulting Group, argues that this disconnect persists because
organizations misdiagnose where the fix belongs:
“Thereʼs got to be a multi-channel assessment optimized
 for leadership and managerial skills, because most of what
we develop is not focused on tracking those skills, tying
them to longitudinal career tracking by linking the skills to
the job architecture, measuring outcomes by connecting
them to business outcomes, and better reporting, using
 skills intelligence to tie it to workforce planning.”

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 Based on our research, please consider the following suggestions:
 1. Fix the systems-alignment problem, not just the training problem.
 Two of the top three goals of performance reviews are helping employees learn and grow and boosting
communication, yet the technology most organizations use rarely supports either. As one contributor
put it, this gap tends to get treated as a training issue when itʼs really an HR systems-alignment issue:
adding a course doesnʼt help if the PM system itself was never built to reinforce it.
 2. Prioritize platforms enabling recognition, ongoing check-ins, and multi-source feedback.
 PM leader organizations are three times as likely to have technology that aids learning and
development (54% vs. 18%) and more than twice as likely to support meeting and check-in tracking
(58% vs. 27%), compared to laggards. This is one of the clearest technology differentiators between
leader and laggard organizations.
 3. Match technology investment to organization size and scalability needs.
 Realistically, many organizations wonʼt have a large budget specifically dedicated to PM technology, but
that doesnʼt mean they should compromise on quality. Smaller organizations especially should seek
out scalable solutions that offer the same efficiency and development benefits as larger enterprise
systems, minus the enterprise-level price tag. The right platform should be able to grow alongside your
organization.

 Among organizations that do not have a culture conducive to

(77%) remains the biggest barrier. A large proportion also say managers

 the time to effectively lead performance (71%), and thereʼs resistance to
 change from employees or management (71%).

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 Based on our research, please consider the following suggestions:
 1. Tackle multiple barriers at once, not just the most visible one.
 Performance leadership rarely stalls for a single reason. When several barriers are working against
 it simultaneously, a response aimed at just one wonʼt be enough to shift the needle. Design
 interventions broad enough to address the real mix of obstacles at play.
 2. Push top leadership to back the important work.
 Culture-building and leadership development are easy to deprioritize when they compete with whatever
feels most pressing in the moment. Senior leadership needs to actively protect space for this longer-
term work, or it will keep losing out to short-term demands.
 3. Close the gap between stated culture and actual follow-through.
 Almost 60% agree their culture supports performance leadership, but only 14% strongly agree, and the
 gap often isnʼt opposition so much as encouragement without systems or follow-through to back it
 up. Audit whether your systems and workload actually reinforce the growth mindset the organization
claims to value.

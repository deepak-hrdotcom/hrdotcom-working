 Major Research Findings
Compensation is becoming more strategic, but relatively few
Major
organizations have developed highly mature total rewards programs.
 1 Finding  
 ●   Only 27% report advanced or innovative compensation and total
rewards maturity.
 ●   Nearly two-fifths (39%) describe their approach as intermediate.
 ●   One-third remain in the beginning or undeveloped stages.
 ●   The proportion reporting an advanced approach has nearly tripled
from 7% in 2022 to 20% in 2026.
Retaining talent remains the primary driver of compensation strategy as
Major
organizations continue modernizing their rewards programs.
 2 Finding  
 ●   Almost three-fifths (57%) identify retaining key talent as their top
 compensation objective.
 ●   Three-fifths (59%) have updated compensation practices within the
 past two years.
 ●   Attracting talent (37%), motivating performance (36%), and ensuring
fair and equitable pay (36%) remain major strategic priorities.
 Organizations continue investing in foundational total rewards, but
personalization and continuous evaluation remain limited.
Major
 3 Finding  
 ●   Among respondents who identified attracting or retaining key talent as a
 top objective:
   Eight in ten offer employee healthcare plans.
    Seven in ten identify fair and equitable pay as a core element of
their rewards offering.
    Two-thirds provide retirement and financial well-being benefits.
 ●   Among respondents who identified enhancing the employee
 experience as a top compensation objective, 35% regularly evaluate
 reward program effectiveness, while just 27% offer flexible benefits
 or lifestyle accounts tailored to employee needs.

 Organizations are preparing for a more data-driven and AI-enabled future
while focusing on maintaining competitive compensation.
Major
 4 Finding
 ●   More than half (52%) identify remaining competitive as their top
compensation priority over the next two years.
 ●   Almost two-fifths (38%) plan to strengthen pay-for-performance
 alignment.
 ●   More than half (55%) see AI's greatest value in automating
 compensation processes, while 51% see opportunities to improve
market analysis and competitive pay decisions.
 TR leaders consistently outperform TR laggard organizations by
adopting data-driven and employee-centric compensation strategies.
Major
 5 Finding  
 Compared to TR laggards, TR leader organizations are:
●   more than 10X more likely to report highly effective compensation
strategies
 ●   6X more likely to regularly evaluate the effectiveness of their total
rewards programs
 ●   more likely to conduct comprehensive pay fairness reviews and
communicate pay transparency beyond legal requirements
 ●   more likely to measure a broader range of compensation
outcomes including offer acceptance, employee retention, market
competitiveness, and pay equity
 ●   delivering a broader employee value proposition, investing more
 heavily in career development, wellness, retirement benefits,
flexible work, tuition assistance, and ongoing total rewards
 communication than their lower-performing counterparts

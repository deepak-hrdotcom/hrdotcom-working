 Addressing Compensation Challenges
Through Fair and Data-Driven Practices
 Organizations face growing pressure to maintain competitive compensation
 programs while managing financial constraints and responding to
 increasing employee expectations. As compensation decisions become
more complex, organizations are placing greater emphasis on fair pay
practices, market data, and performance measurement to guide their
strategies.
 Finding: Budget constraints and rising
employee expectations continue to challenge
compensation decisions
 Organizations face competing pressures when designing compensation
programs. Budget constraints remain the most common challenge (44%),
followed by weak links between pay and performance (30%), wage compression
 (28%), and difficulty personalizing rewards to employee needs (27%).

 Finding: Nearly three-fifths of organizations
 track pay ranges
 Many organizations have implemented practices to improve compensation
 fairness and consistency. Among respondents who identified providing fair
 and equitable pay as one of their organization's top three compensation
and total rewards objectives, the most common initiative is complying with
 pay transparency laws (77%), followed by conducting periodic reviews to
evaluate pay fairness (62%) and using real-time market data to benchmark
and adjust pay (54%). Nearly half of organizations incorporate fair pay
 into their compensation philosophy (49%) and actively manage employee
perceptions of pay equity (43%).
Organizations primarily measure metrics that support compensation
 planning and market positioning. The most commonly tracked metric is
pay range (61%), followed by salary competitiveness (51%) and total cost
of the workforce (48%). Many organizations also monitor compa-ratio
(44%), range width (37%), and internal equity metrics (36%), indicating
 a focus on maintaining consistent pay structures and competitive
compensation practices.
 Differences by organizational size
 Small organizations are more likely to track the total cost of the workforce
 (59%) than salary competitiveness (46%) or pay ranges (49%), reflecting
 a stronger focus on managing compensation costs. Larger organizations,
however, are more likely to measure salary competitiveness, internal
equity, market ratios, geographic differentials, and offer acceptance rates.

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 Based on our research consider the following suggestions:
 ●   Find ways to balance financial realities with employee expectations.
 Budget constraints remain the biggest challenge, while employees continue to feel the impact of
 rising living costs and insufficient wage growth. Review your compensation strategy to identify
where limited resources can have the greatest impact. When significant pay increases aren't
possible, consider strengthening other parts of your total rewards package, such as flexible work
arrangements, recognition programs, career development, or well-being initiatives to better support
 employees.
 ●   Make fair pay an ongoing practice, not a one-time review.
 Many organizations are taking steps to improve pay fairness, but more mature organizations go
further by regularly reviewing pay equity, using current market data, and communicating their
approach to employees. Schedule regular pay equity reviews, update market benchmarks annually,
 and clearly explain how pay decisions are made to help build employee trust and confidence.
 ●   Measure the impact of your compensation strategy, not just compensation costs.
 Many organizations track salary ranges and workforce costs, but fewer measure whether
compensation is helping attract and retain talent. Expand your reporting to include measures such as
retention related to pay, offer acceptance rates, internal equity, and employee perceptions of fairness.
Reviewing these measures regularly can help identify where compensation programs are delivering
value and where improvements are needed.
 ●   Use compensation data to make better decisions.
 Organizations with more mature compensation strategies rely on a broader range of compensation
metrics to guide their decisions. Bring together salary survey data, internal pay information, workforce
trends, and compensation metrics into simple dashboards that managers and leaders can use
when making compensation decisions. Using reliable data can improve consistency, support market
competitiveness, and reduce bias.

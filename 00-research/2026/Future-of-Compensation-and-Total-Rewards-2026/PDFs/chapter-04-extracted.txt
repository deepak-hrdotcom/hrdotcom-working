 The employee experience extends well beyond compensation alone,
and organizations are increasingly investing in initiatives that strengthen
communication, well-being, and development. The most common initiative
 is ensuring employees have easy access to pay and benefits information
(68%) followed by educating employees about their benefits and total
rewards package (65%), offering wellness programs (65%) and promoting
work-life balance through flexible work arrangements (62%).
At the same time, several important elements of a modern total rewards
strategy remain less common. Fewer than half of organizations offer
 competitive compensation plans (46%), maintain transparency in wage
 and benefits calculations (35%), or regularly evaluate the effectiveness of
 their reward programs (35%).

support for caregiving and remote work, such as caregiver referral services

 uncommon, indicating opportunities to better support employees with
 diverse personal and family responsibilities.

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
Based on our research consider the following suggestions:
 ●   Build on your foundation by offering rewards that meet changing employee needs.
 Most organizations already provide the core rewards employees expect, such as healthcare,
retirement plans, and paid time off. The next opportunity is to make rewards more meaningful
by identifying where employees' needs are changing. Use employee surveys, focus groups, and
workforce data to understand what employees value most, then adjust your rewards package where it
 will have the greatest impact.
 ●   Give employees more choice in how they are rewarded.
 Relatively few organizations offer flexible or personalized rewards, even though employee needs
vary across different life stages and career paths. Where possible, introduce options such as flexible
benefits, lifestyle spending accounts, voluntary benefits, or additional leave choices so employees can
select rewards that best fit their individual needs.
 ●   Regularly evaluate whether your rewards programs are working.
 Although many organizations invest in total rewards, relatively few regularly evaluate their
effectiveness. Establish an annual review process that looks at employee participation, satisfaction,
retention, recruitment outcomes, and business results. Use employee feedback together with program
data to identify which rewards provide the greatest value and where improvements are needed.
 ●   Help employees understand the value of their total rewards.
 Employees cannot fully appreciate benefits they don't understand. Communicate your total rewards
package throughout the year, not just during enrollment periods. Consider providing total rewards
statements, manager toolkits, employee information sessions, and simple explanations of available
programs so employees recognize the full value of what the organization provides.
 ●   Make fairness and employee experience part of your rewards strategy.
 Organizations with more mature total rewards strategies are much more likely to support employee
well-being, regularly evaluate their programs, and provide a better employee experience. Review
 your rewards programs regularly to ensure they are fair, accessible, and aligned with employee
expectations. Pay equity reviews, transparent communication, and ongoing employee feedback can
 help strengthen trust and improve the overall employee experience.

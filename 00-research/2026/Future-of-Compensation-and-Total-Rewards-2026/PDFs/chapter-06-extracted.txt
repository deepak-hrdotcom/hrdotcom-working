 Organizations recognize AI's potential to improve the efficiency and
 effectiveness of compensation management. The greatest perceived

analyzing industry and market trends to support competitive pay (51%),

 while interest in AI is growing, many organizations are still developing

rewards.
 Differences by organizational size
 Organizations of all sizes recognize opportunities for AI, but larger
organizations place greater emphasis on more advanced applications.
Compared with small organizations, they are more likely to see value in
identifying compensation disparities, providing managers with better
decision-making insights, and using predictive analytics to optimize
rewards. Small organizations are comparatively more likely to be
uncertain about where AI can create value, suggesting they are earlier in
their AI adoption journey.

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
Based on our research consider the following suggestions:
 ●   Develop a forward-looking compensation strategy.
 Organizations expect compensation to play an increasingly strategic role in attracting, retaining,
and motivating talent. Review your compensation strategy regularly to ensure it supports
changing business priorities, workforce expectations, and labor market conditions. Consider how
compensation, career development, and total rewards work together to create a compelling employee
value proposition that remains competitive over time.
 ●   Strengthen the connection between pay and performance.
 Aligning pay to performance continues to be one of organizations' highest future priorities. Review
 how performance outcomes influence compensation decisions and ensure managers have clear,
 consistent guidelines for recognizing individual and team contributions. Regularly evaluate whether
performance-based rewards reinforce the behaviors and results that support organizational success.
 ●   Start with practical AI applications that improve efficiency.
 Organizations see the greatest opportunity for AI in automating manual processes, analyzing market
trends, and supporting compensation decisions. Focus initial AI investments on routine, high-value
activities such as market pricing, salary benchmarking, reporting, and compensation analysis
before expanding into more advanced applications. Starting with practical use cases can help build
 confidence while delivering measurable efficiency gains.
 ●   Maintain human oversight when using AI.
 While AI can improve the speed and quality of compensation analysis, compensation decisions
should continue to involve human judgment. Establish clear governance for AI use by defining where
 AI supports decision-making, validating outputs with reliable data, and ensuring managers retain
 responsibility for final compensation decisions. This approach can help improve consistency while
 reducing the risk of bias or unintended outcomes.
 ●   Build organizational readiness for AI in compensation.
 Many organizations are still determining where AI can create the greatest value, particularly smaller
organizations. Before implementing new AI tools, assess your organization's readiness by identifying
 priority use cases, evaluating data quality, developing internal capabilities, and providing training
 for HR and compensation professionals. A phased approach will help organizations adopt AI more
effectively while supporting long-term success.

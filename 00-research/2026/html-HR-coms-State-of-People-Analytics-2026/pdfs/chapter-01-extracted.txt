 Executive Summary
 Organizations have access to more workforce data than ever, yet many still struggle to
turn that information into meaningful business action. Findings from HR.com’s State
of People Analytics 2026 report, informed by a three-round Delphi study and advisory
board discussions with senior people analytics practitioners and industry experts, reveal
a persistent execution gap. When asked which single barrier would unlock the greatest
organizational impact if completely solved, 57% of experts identify this execution gap as
 the primary challenge facing the field.
The findings suggest that organizations no longer struggle primarily with data access or
 reporting capability. Instead, the larger challenge is decision activation, which refers to
partnering directly with business leaders to turn workforce insights into concrete action
plans, operational changes, and measurable follow-through. Half of the experts view this
as the key to closing the execution gap.
 The study also shows that people analytics increasingly gains influence through
 business relevance and operational integration rather than technical sophistication
 alone. Experts define high-impact analytics teams as those embedded into routine
 leadership and operational planning discussions, where workforce insights directly
shape business decisions. At the same time, 50% of experts state that the biggest
barrier keeping analytics teams out of operational planning meetings is lack of
 relevance, meaning workforce metrics are not tied closely enough to financial or
 operational goals.
 The findings also reveal growing caution around large-scale AI expansion. While AI
 is expected to enhance analytics, experts stress the continued importance of human
judgment and context. With limited resources, 92% recommend piloting AI in targeted
use cases rather than scaling broadly.
Ultimately, 43% of experts conclude that the primary role of the people analytics
function over the next two years must evolve into that of a Strategic Advisor that guides
executive strategy with workforce insights and research. Future competitive advantage
will depend more on helping organizations act effectively on the information they
already have.
 We examine these and other insights in our summary of key findings below.

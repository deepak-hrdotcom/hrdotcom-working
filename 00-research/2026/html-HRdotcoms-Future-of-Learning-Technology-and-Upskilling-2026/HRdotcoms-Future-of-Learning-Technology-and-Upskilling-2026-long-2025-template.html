HR.com’s State of Legal Compliance and Employment Law 2025

Executive Summary

As employment laws grow more complex, many organizations remain unprepared. Only one-third of responding organizations take a proactive approach to labor law compliance, according to our 2025 research. Meanwhile, 22% do not take proactive steps, 20% have outdated processes, 16% have only partially documented procedures, and 9% remain purely reactive.

This report, which focuses primarily on U.S.-focused compliance issues, helps HR professionals navigate this complex environment by addressing:

  • gaps in compliance processes, risk management, and audit frequency
  • top compliance concerns, including pay, leave, and benefits laws
    • resource and funding shortfalls impacting readiness
    • technology limitations and the evolving role of AI in compliance
  • legal, financial, and reputational risks of falling behind

To offer you a succinct overview of the report's contents, we have distilled the key findings below.

Major Research Findings

1. Despite the dire consequences of non-compliance, organizations struggle with providing adequate support for the issue.

  • Lawsuits (69%) and large fines (61%) are the most frequently cited risks related to non-compliance.
  • Damage to an organization’s reputation is another major concern for half of the respondents.
  • Over a fifth of organizations (22%) struggle to keep up-to-date with compliance, rating six or less on a 10-point scale.
  • Further, organizations struggle to provide adequate amounts of resources. Under half of respondents say all stakeholders receive adequate:
    • education and training (46%)
    • tools and technologies (39%)
    • support (such as budget) (35%)

Current State of Compliance Readiness

Finding: Gaps in proactive legal compliance leave organizations vulnerable

We asked respondents to rate their organizations on the employment and labor law compliance maturity model (outlined in graph below). Although a third of organizations take a proactive approach to labor law compliance, two-thirds operate with some level of deficiency. This was about the same proportion of responses in last year’s research on the same topic.

Compliance cohort classification

To take a closer look at what differentiates organizations with more mature compliance processes from those with less mature compliance processes, we separated our sample into two cohorts:

  • Compliance leaders: Those who are at the “proactive” stage of employment and labor law compliance maturity.
  • Compliance laggards: Those who are at the “reactive”, “formalized,” or “documented” stage of employment and labor law compliance maturity.

Of course, correlation is not the same as causation. While we cannot state that any particular practice will lead to success in managing legal compliance, we do see intriguing relationships that might result in greater success.

Results of Chi-square Test

A chi-square test of independence shows that compliance leaders are significantly more likely than laggards to say their compliance-related initiatives are well-funded and they are well prepared to deal with labor and employment compliance concerns.

HRRI Strategic Recommendations

Based on our research, please consider these suggestions:

  • Adopt proactive compliance strategies. Conduct regular risk assessments, use compliance software for tracking legal updates, and establish a task force to review policies regularly.
  • Prioritize pay transparency training. Host workshops for HR and managers, implement a clear pay transparency policy, and conduct regular pay audits to ensure fairness.
  • Increase funding for compliance. Use risk data to secure a dedicated budget, integrate compliance funding into the annual budget, and assign a compliance officer for oversight.
  • Address under-prioritized compliance areas. Provide training on discrimination and immigration laws, establish a reporting system, and collaborate with legal experts to update policies.
  • Position compliance as a leadership priority. Include compliance goals in leadership reviews, form a compliance committee, and hold regular briefings for senior leaders.

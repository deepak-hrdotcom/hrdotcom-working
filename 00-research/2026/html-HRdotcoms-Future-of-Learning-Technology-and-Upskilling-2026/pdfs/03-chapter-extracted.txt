 Finding: A third of respondents say that
upskilling is not a priority for top leadership
 Perceptions of the importance of upskilling vary across stakeholder
groups. Employees place the greatest urgency on development, with 35%
ranking upskilling as their top priority or among their top three priorities.
Executives report a similar level of urgency, with 35% also placing
upskilling within their top three priorities, although 32% do not consider it
a priority at all.
One possible explanation is that many leaders remain focused on
 immediate operational and financial priorities, making investments in
workforce development more difficult to justify despite growing evidence
 that skill shortages and technological change are increasing the need
for continuous learning. HR professionals appear somewhat less likely
to view upskilling as a top-tier issue, with fewer ranking it among their
highest priorities.
 These findings suggest that while a segment of leaders recognize
 the strategic importance of upskilling, employees may feel a stronger
need for development opportunities and clearer pathways for skill
growth. Mercer's comprehensive evaluation outlines a structural “triple
misalignment” among employees, HR, and the C-suite. Their data
 confirms that most employees are deeply concerned about lacking
 future-ready skills, to the extent that 63% of workers would willingly trade
a 10% pay raise for guaranteed upskilling opportunities.
Organizations that successfully position upskilling as a business priority
 rather than an employee benefit may be better equipped to secure
 executive support and sustained investment.

and deploy talent effectively. When learning, recruitment, performance
management, and career development operate separately, organizations may
struggle to connect skill development with workforce planning and internal
mobility goals. To address this strategic misalignment, McKinsey & Company
recommends aligning business, HR, and technology leaders around critical
skills rather than managing learning in functional silos.

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 Based on our research, please consider the following suggestions:
 1. Connect learning initiatives to business priorities
 Learning programs are more likely to gain leadership support when they address specific business
 challenges. Rather than building programs around available content, focus development efforts on the
skills and capabilities that will have the greatest impact on organizational success. Consider:
 ●   identifying the top workforce challenges facing the organization
 ●   prioritizing development efforts that address critical skill gaps
 ●   aligning learning objectives with business goals
 ●   regularly communicating the business value of learning initiatives
 2. Make manager development a top priority
 Managers have a significant influence on employee performance, engagement, and growth. Investing
 in manager capability can improve team effectiveness while strengthening the overall learning culture.
Consider:
 ●   providing leadership development for new and experienced managers
 ●   strengthening coaching and feedback skills
 ●   helping managers identify and address skill gaps within their teams
 ●   equipping managers to support employee development conversations
 3. Align learning with talent management processes
 Learning should not operate independently from hiring, performance management, succession
planning, and career development. Connecting these processes helps ensure development efforts
support broader talent goals. Consider:
 ●   partnering with talent acquisition and talent management teams
 ●   aligning learning pathways with career progression opportunities
 ●   incorporating skill development into performance discussions
 ●   using workforce planning data to inform learning priorities
 4. Measure whether learning is making a difference
 Tracking course completions and participation rates is important, but it does not show whether
learning is improving workforce capability. Focus on measures that demonstrate impact. Consider:
 ●   assessing whether critical skill gaps are decreasing
 ●   tracking internal promotions and mobility
 ●   evaluating manager and leadership readiness
 ●   gathering feedback on how learning is being applied on the job

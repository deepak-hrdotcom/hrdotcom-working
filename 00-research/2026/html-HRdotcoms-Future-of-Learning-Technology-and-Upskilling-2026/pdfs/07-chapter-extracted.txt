 Conclusion
 Organizations increasingly recognize that employee skill-building is
essential for keeping pace with changing business needs and new
technologies. However, many are still struggling to translate that
commitment into meaningful results. Despite growing investments in
learning technology and AI, learning often remains disconnected from
business priorities and day-to-day work.
Future success will depend less on offering more training and more
on helping employees continuously build and apply new skills.
Organizations that focus learning on real business challenges, support
managers in employee development, and make learning easier to access
during the workday will be better prepared to address skill gaps and
adapt to change.
Technology is also changing how employees learn. Looking ahead, David
Creelman, CEO of Creelman Research, predicts that learning will become
much more conversational and immediate.
 “Most learning will come directly from chatting with a large language
model with only a bit of direction from L&D on how to engage with the
model.” – David Creelman

 However, the experts caution that simply producing more content with AI is
unlikely to create better outcomes. Instead, L&D professionals must rethink how
learning is delivered and measured. As Creelman advises to   “invest in discovering
how AI transforms how learning can be delivered, don't just use AI to generate a lot
more learning content.”
 Similarly, Matthew Casey-Sudvary emphasizes the importance of starting with
business outcomes rather than technology adoption. He says,   “Define the problem
and how you will know your program/tech is a success then measure and report.”
 Perhaps the biggest shift will be the evolution of L&D itself. JD Dillon argues that
capability building can no longer operate as an isolated function:
 “Break out of the L&D silo and adopt an enablement mindset in collaboration with
functions like communications, knowledge management, IT, and operations.”
 – JD Dillon
 Becky Willis provides pragmatic advice for teams trying to update their
infrastructure, warning professionals to stop getting stuck in legacy upgrade
cycles:   “Stop looking for a new LMS. Find a new solution that can either hit all the
AI checkmarks by itself or add it on top of your dinosaur LMS.”   She also cautions
 leaders to beware of red flags from vendor AI claims and strongly advises them to
 get help when navigating this shifting market.
As the pace of change continues to increase, the role of L&D will shift from
delivering training to helping employees learn, adapt, and perform more effectively
every day. Organizations that embed learning into daily tools, hold managers
accountable for team development, and measure skill gaps instead of course
completions will close capability gaps faster than those still waiting to act.

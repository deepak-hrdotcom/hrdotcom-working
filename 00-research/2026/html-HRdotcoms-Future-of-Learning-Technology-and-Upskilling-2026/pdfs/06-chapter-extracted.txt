 Finding: A quarter of organizations report that
their AI reskilling strategy is currently under
development
 As AI adoption accelerates, workforce readiness is emerging as a key
 barrier to transformation. The challenge is no longer recognizing the need
for AI skills, but building the processes and learning infrastructure required
 to develop them at scale.
 When asked about their organization’s current approach to reskilling
 employees in response to AI, a quarter (26%) report that their AI reskilling
strategy is currently under development, making it the most common
 response. Many organizations are likely still in the early stages because
AI technologies, required skills, and business use cases continue to
evolve rapidly, making it difficult to identify future skill needs and develop
comprehensive, organization-wide reskilling programs.
 An additional 23% report having a mix of formal and informal initiatives
 in place, while 17% have established a formal strategy and 19% have no
strategy at all. Together, these findings suggest that most organizations
have begun taking steps to prepare their workforces for the impact of AI,
 even if many efforts remain in the early stages of maturity.

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 Based on our research, please consider the following suggestions:
 1. Build a structured AI workforce readiness strategy
 Many organizations recognize the importance of AI but are still developing formal approaches to
 workforce preparation. A clear strategy can help ensure employees develop the skills needed to work
effectively alongside AI. Consider:
 ●   defining AI capabilities required across different roles
 ●   creating role-based learning pathways
 ●   establishing workforce readiness goals
 ●   aligning AI skill development with business priorities
 2. Focus AI on workforce capability, not just productivity
 Many organizations are successfully using AI to improve efficiency and access to information. The
 next opportunity is using AI to strengthen skills, support career growth, and improve workforce
readiness. Consider:
 ●   identifying workforce challenges AI can help address
 ●   supporting personalized learning and development
 ●   exploring AI-enabled coaching and learning support
 ●   evaluating workforce outcomes alongside productivity gains
 3. Create opportunities for employees to learn by doing
 AI capabilities are often developed through experimentation and real-world application. Employees
 need opportunities to explore tools, test ideas, and share successful practices. Consider:
 ●   providing safe environments to test AI tools
 ●   encouraging peer learning and knowledge sharing
 ●   incorporating practical exercises into AI training
 ●   showcasing successful AI applications across the organization

 4. Strengthen governance as AI adoption expands

 increasingly important. Governance helps organizations manage risk while supporting innovation.

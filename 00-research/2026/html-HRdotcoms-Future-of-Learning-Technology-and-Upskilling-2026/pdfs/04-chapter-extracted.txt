 Finding: Almost three-fifths of respondents
report that employees engage in upskilling
on an on-demand or as-needed basis
 Employee learning is driven primarily by immediate needs rather than
 ongoing development. Almost three-fifths (58%) report that employees
 engage in upskilling on an on-demand or as-needed basis, while
 only 18% say employees participate in continuous learning. Smaller
percentages report learning taking place multiple times a year (10%) or
according to a more structured schedule.
While on-demand learning can help employees address immediate skill
needs, it may do little to build future capabilities or prepare for emerging
workforce demands. At the same time,   growing workloads and rising
 expectations leave employees with little time to pause, reflect, or engage
 in deeper learning.
 Encouraging more continuous skill development could help employees
build capabilities over time while improving organizational readiness for
future changes in skills and work.

 Finding: Forty-five percent of respondents
report that learning is only partially
integrated into employees' daily workflows
 Learning in the flow of work remains more aspiration than reality for
many organizations. While 45% of respondents report that learning is
partially embedded into daily workflows, only 11% say learning is fully
integrated into the tools and processes employees use every day. At
the same time, 33% indicate that learning remains largely separate
from daily work, and another 10% report that their organizations do not
support learning in the flow of work at all.
These findings suggest that many organizations still rely on traditional
learning approaches that require employees to step away from their work
 to learn. However,   research suggests learning is most effective when
it occurs in the flow of work, making integration into existing tools and
workflows increasingly important.

 HRRI Strategic Recommendations
 Based on our research, please consider the following suggestions:
 1. Create more opportunities for continuous learning
 Many employees develop skills only when an immediate need arises. Organizations can build
workforce readiness by making learning a regular part of the employee experience rather than an
activity reserved for specific projects or skill gaps. Consider:
 ●   encouraging ongoing skill development throughout the year
 ●   incorporating development goals into regular employee conversations
 ●   providing employees with visibility into future skill requirements
 ●   supporting self-directed learning aligned with career growth opportunities
 2. Build on the strength of peer learning
 Employees are already teaching and learning from one another. Organizations can amplify these
behaviors by creating more structured opportunities for knowledge sharing and collaboration.
Consider:
 ●   establishing mentoring and peer coaching programs
 ●   creating communities of practice around key skill areas
 ●   recognizing employees who share expertise with others
 ●   encouraging cross-functional learning and collaboration
 3. Give employees time and support to learn
 Learning is difficult to sustain when employees are expected to develop new skills on top of full
workloads. Organizations should ensure employees have both the time and managerial support
needed to learn effectively. Consider:
 ●   dedicating time for learning during the workweek
 ●   encouraging managers to discuss development regularly
 ●   setting realistic expectations for learning participation
 ●   helping managers balance operational demands with employee growth

 4. Bring learning closer to the flow of work
 Employees are more likely to develop new skills when learning resources are available at the
moment they need them. Embedding learning into everyday work can improve both participation and
knowledge application. Consider:
 ●   integrating learning resources into commonly used tools and systems
 ●   providing just-in-time learning opportunities
 ●   connecting learning content to real work challenges
 ●   making it easy for employees to access learning without leaving their workflow
 5. Create clearer development pathways
 Employees are more likely to invest in learning when they understand how skill development
 supports career growth. Organizations should help employees connect learning activities to future
opportunities. Consider:
 ●   defining skill requirements for key roles
 ●   communicating potential career pathways
 ●   linking learning opportunities to advancement opportunities
 ●   helping employees identify development priorities

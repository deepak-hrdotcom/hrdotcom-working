 Our Major Research Findings
 Organizations recognize that upskilling is a strategic priority, but most
Major
have yet to build highly effective learning functions.
 1 Finding
 ●   Only 30% rate their L&D efforts as highly effective.
 ●   Two-thirds expect employee upskilling to become more or much more
important over the next two years.
 ●   Only 17% report that learning is fully aligned with talent acquisition,
succession planning, and performance management.
 ●   Nearly one-third (32%) say upskilling is not a priority for top leadership,
highlighting a disconnect between business needs and executive
commitment.
 Skills gaps remain widespread, yet learning continues to be reactive rather
Major
than continuous.
2 Finding
 ●   56% report frequent skills gaps among mid-level managers, the
highest of any employee group.
 ●   58% say employees upskill only when an immediate need arises.
 ●   Only 18% report that employees engage in continuous learning.
 ●   Just 37% say employees are given dedicated time to learn during the
workday.

 Learning technology is widely adopted, but fragmented systems limit its
strategic value.
Major
 3 Finding
 ●   67% use a Learning Management System (LMS) or Learning
Experience Platform (LXP).
 ●   AI-powered learning tools are one of the fastest-growing learning
technologies with 37% currently adopting them versus 15% last year.
 ●   Only 37% report their primary LMS is fully integrated with their HR
system and just 44% say learning systems integrate well with HR and
other enterprise platforms.
 ●   The biggest technology weaknesses are lack of integration into the
 flow of work (39%), lack of personalization (37%), and poor integration
 with other systems (32%).
 Learning technologies perform well for administration and compliance but
fall short in building workforce capability.
Major
4 Finding
 ●   88% rate their learning technologies as effective for compliance and
regulatory training.
 ●   Only 13% say their learning technologies are extremely effective
at closing skill gaps and just 14% say the same about delivering
personalized learning experiences.
 ●   Nearly two-thirds (65%) measure learning success through immediate
knowledge or skill improvement, while only 28% formally calculate
learning return on investment (ROI).
 ●   Just 11% say learning is fully embedded into employees' daily
 workflows.

 AI is transforming workplace learning, but organizational readiness
remains in the early stages.
Major
 5 Finding
 ●   72% use AI to help employees search and access organizational
knowledge.
 ●   56% use AI for administrative automation or content generation, while
52% provide personalized learning recommendations.
 ●   26% report their AI reskilling strategy is still under development.
 ●   Only 17% have a formal AI reskilling strategy in place and 19% still
have no strategy.
 ●   74% develop AI skills through supported hands-on experimentation,
yet only 17% are actively researching or evaluating agentic AI for L&D.

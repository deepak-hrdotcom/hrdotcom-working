# Research Survey Emailer Template Strategy

## Campaign Thinking

The goal of these four emailer templates is to help future research campaigns drive more clicks and survey completions. Each template approaches the same goal from a different angle: curiosity, personal relevance, reminder urgency, and executive-level problem framing. This gives stakeholders four reusable options rather than four versions of the same message, and it gives each campaign more flexibility depending on audience type, topic, and send sequence.

## Emailer A: Broad Research Invitation

Emailer A is the strongest general-audience opener. It is designed for campaigns where we have a clear benchmark, statistic, or industry gap that can create immediate curiosity. The message is direct, easy to scan, and positions the survey as a quick way to contribute to useful HR.com research. The CTA style is designed to reduce friction by making the action feel manageable.

## Emailer B: Warm Community Invitation

Emailer B is built for recipients who have already engaged with HR.com content, webcasts, or previous surveys related to the campaign topic. The thought process here is to make the recipient feel specifically chosen, not broadly marketed to. This template works well when the campaign can reference a known challenge or audience-relevant issue and frame participation as sharing a valuable perspective. The CTA style feels more personal and less transactional.

## Emailer C: Reminder Nudge

Emailer C is intended as a follow-up or reminder template. Instead of leading with a statistic, it focuses on timing: there is still time to add your perspective before the research is finalized. This version is shorter, warmer, and more action-oriented, making it useful for people who may have seen an earlier message but did not respond. The CTA style reinforces that the recipient's input still matters.

## Emailer D: CRM Executive Brief

Emailer D is the more formal CRM template for engaged contacts or a more decision-maker-oriented audience. It frames the survey around identifying the gaps, risks, or priorities that matter most to the topic. This makes the message feel more strategic and business-focused. The issue-map structure helps explain why the input matters without making the email feel long. The CTA style is direct and appropriate for a polished CRM send.

HR.com's Future of Human Experience	Wednesday, April 8, 2026	Employee expectations are rising. Burnout remains a threat. Artificial intelligence is changing how work gets done. Yet many organizations still struggle to create a work experience that truly supports engagement, well-being, growth, and performance. Join us as we unpack the findings from HR.com’s 7th Annual Future of Human Experience Study 2026 and explore what separates organizations that are making real progress from those that are falling behind. This webcast will look at how human resources leaders rate the employee experience today, what they see as the strongest drivers of a positive workplace, and where the biggest problems continue to hold people and organizations back. We will examine the workplace factors that matter most, from relationships with coworkers and managers to purpose, flexibility, career growth, workload, recognition, and pay. We will also explore how organizations are measuring employee experience, which tools and practices they find most effective, and how flexible work models continue to shape the day-to-day reality of work. We will also look at the barriers slowing progress, including budget limits, competing priorities, leadership buy-in, and gaps in awareness or infrastructure. IIn addition, we will discuss the growing role of AI in the employee experience. The research looks at where AI may improve productivity, communication, learning, and personalization, as well as where it may raise concerns around privacy, trust, and the human side of work. Throughout, we will highlight the differences between organizations that excel at human experience practices those that do not, searching for strategies that can lead to greater success. Will also intersperse our research findings with practical ways of leveraging those findings. Join Mark Vickers, Chief Research Analyst and Data Wrangler, as he interprets the findings and shares practical insights for building a more engaging, effective, and human-centered workplace. As a webcast participant, you will also receive a copy of the research report based on this exclusive HR.com study.
